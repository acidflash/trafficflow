# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Network administrators at a small operator (AXNET) who run a fibre access network: MikroTik SwOS switches out to apartments and customers, a CCR2216 core router, and upstream peering. The same people look at the map in every setting: on a wall screen in operations where it must read at a glance from across the room, on a laptop while troubleshooting a link, on a phone on the way to or inside a technical room, and at night on call in a dark room where the screen must not glare.

## Product Purpose

Trafficflow shows the live topology of the network and how much traffic each link carries, with 30 days of history. It answers three questions, in order: is anything down, is anything close to full, and where does this traffic go. Success is that an administrator can glance at the map and know the state of the network, then drill into a link or device without leaving the map. The app only reads (SNMP); it never changes device configuration.

## Brand Personality

Operations centre: calm, dense, precise. The interface is quiet when the network is healthy and only draws the eye when something needs attention. It speaks Swedish, short and technical, like a colleague on call. Numbers and names come first; decoration never competes with them.

## Anti-references

No specific anti-references were given. Avoid anything that makes a healthy network look alarming, and anything that hides the topology behind panels.

## Design Principles

1. **Quiet when healthy.** Neutral surfaces and muted links by default; colour is spent on load, faults and selection, so it always means something.
2. **Topology first.** The map is the product. Side panels support it and never crowd it; every element on the map encodes network state.
3. **Readable at any distance.** Rates, names and status must hold up on a wall screen across the room and on a phone in a technical room.
4. **Night-safe.** Designed for long sessions in a dark room: low glare, no large bright surfaces.
5. **Say what it is.** Routers, switches and external networks are visibly different things; icons and shapes follow the equipment, not generic app metaphors.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No colour-vision requirement from the team; load and status may rely on colour. Keep text contrast at WCAG AA or better on the dark surfaces, keep keyboard focus visible, and respect reduced motion.
